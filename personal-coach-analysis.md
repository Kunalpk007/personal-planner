# A Coach's Read on Kunal's Planner

You asked for this as a coach would give it, not as an engineer would: is the system you've built actually going to make you more disciplined and consistent, keep you honestly motivated, and show you where your time is really going — and is there anything in it that's just noise. I read through the whole app to answer that honestly, not from a feature checklist. Here's where it stands.

## What's already working in your favor

The core loop is genuinely well built for behavior change, not just task-checking. The streak system protects you with a rest day when you miss instead of just punishing you, and — after this week's fix — it also lets you correct an honest mistake within a fair window, with an explicit "this is for real misses, not padding your streak" line right where you'd be tempted to cheat. That's a real coaching instinct: protect the streak from bad luck, guard it against dishonesty, and say so out loud. Most habit apps only do one of those two things.

The mood-aware scoring is another strength worth naming, because it's easy to miss how unusual it is. Most task apps hold you to the same bar every day regardless of how you're actually doing; yours lowers the bar on a genuinely bad day (sick mode) and raises the ceiling on a good one (motivated mode), with a built-in allowance so "sick" can't quietly become a permanent excuse. That's the difference between a system that demands willpower and one that works with the energy you actually have.

You've also built real social accountability — friends, challenges, and a validation/approval layer so a friend can sign off on a task or a reward before it counts — which is one of the highest-leverage things in behavior science and almost never shows up in a personal planner. And the wellness layer (breathing exercise, water tracking, a blocking full-screen focus timer) means the app isn't only asking "did you produce," it's also asking "are you okay while producing." That combination is rare enough that I'd call it the app's actual strength, more than any single feature.

## The gap that matters most: you're tracking task completion, not time or attention

Go back to your own words: you wanted "a tracker of the things you are doing" and confirmation that "you're not wasting time and being unproductive." Right now, the app can't actually answer that question, because nothing in it measures time or effort — only whether a checkbox got ticked. A five-minute task and a three-hour task earn points purely by priority label (high/medium/low), not by how long they actually took or how much attention they demanded. Focus Time comes closest, but it only logs a fixed 25/45/60-minute session you chose in advance — it's not a log of where your actual hours went, and it's entirely opt-in, so a day can go by without it ever being touched.

The practical effect is that you could have a genuinely unproductive, scattered day — real time lost to distraction — and still see a perfect streak and full points, as long as the tasks on the list happened to get checked off. The system has no way to catch that, because it was never asked to look for it. If "am I actually wasting time" is a goal you care about as much as "did I do my tasks," that needs its own signal — something as simple as an end-of-day one-line prompt ("what pulled your focus today?") or a lightweight actual-duration field on tasks, not just a priority-based point value. That's the single highest-leverage addition I'd make, because it's the one thing you explicitly asked for that the app currently has no mechanism for at all.

## A ritual you already half-built and never turned on

While reading the code I found something worth flagging directly: there's already a `setWeeklyReviewDone` action in your data layer that takes a date and a free-text reflection — the plumbing for a weekly review exists — but no screen anywhere in the app ever calls it. It's dead code, tested but never surfaced. That's a nearly-free win: a five-minute Sunday prompt ("what worked this week, what didn't, what will you change") is one of the most consistently effective discipline habits in any serious productivity system, and you already have the trend charts and Life Score data to make that review genuinely informative instead of generic. Right now the app has all the ingredients for reflection and none of the moment that asks you to actually do it.

In the same vein, there's no explicit "decide tomorrow's most important task tonight" ritual. "Today's Focus" on the dashboard is auto-picked from whatever's still unfinished — it's not something you deliberately committed to the evening before, when you have the clarity that's usually gone by the next morning. A short end-of-day prompt to name tomorrow's one non-negotiable task would close that gap and costs very little to build.

## Where the app may be doing more than it needs to

You also asked what's not required, and I want to be straight about this rather than just add more. Right now the dashboard runs five parallel progress systems at once — Rank/XP ladder, streak plus freeze tokens, badges, a reward wallet, and Life Score — all visible, all with their own numbers. Each one is well designed individually, but together they risk the exact opposite of what you're going for: instead of one clear "am I doing well today," you have five different metrics competing for a glance. If motivation ever starts to feel like bookkeeping instead of momentum, this is where I'd look first — not to delete anything, but to consider which one or two numbers should lead on the dashboard and let the rest live one tap deeper, in history or settings.

I'd also gently flag the slot-mismatch penalty — the −20% hit for completing a task outside its assigned time-of-day block. It's a reasonable idea in principle, but it's the same shape of rule that caused the original rest-day trust problem this session started with: a rigid, well-intentioned rule that can end up punishing someone for doing the work, just not exactly on schedule. Worth watching whether it's actually changing your behavior for the better or just quietly encouraging you to mislabel when you did something.

Finally, the validation/notary system — friends approving your tasks or reward redemptions — is genuinely clever, but it's also the most complex thing in the app, and its value depends entirely on whether you and your friends actually use it day to day. If it's sitting unused, that's fine to leave as-is since it costs you nothing when idle; if it's adding friction to redeeming a reward you've honestly earned, it's worth reconsidering whether it needs to be on by default.

## Where I'd focus next, in order

If I had to rank it: first, give yourself a real signal for time and attention, not just task completion — that's the one gap that maps directly to what you said you wanted and don't have today. Second, turn on the weekly review that's already half-built, paired with a tonight-decide-tomorrow's-priority prompt — both are cheap to add and are exactly the kind of ritual that turns a tracking app into a discipline habit. Third, take an honest look at whether five simultaneous progress systems are helping you focus or splitting your attention, and consider quieting the dashboard down to whichever one or two actually move you. Everything else in the app — the streak protection, the mood-aware targets, the social accountability, the wellness layer — is already doing real work for you and I wouldn't touch it.
